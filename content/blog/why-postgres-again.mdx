Why is everyone suddenly interested in a 30-year-old database?

Last month, Databricks acquired Neon for a whopping $1B. Just days later, Snowflake snapped up Crunchy Data. Both are Postgres startups. And no — this isn’t a nostalgia play.

It’s a signal. Something fundamental is changing in how AI systems use data.

## The AI shift: from users to agents

In the old world, humans queried databases.

In the new world, AI agents spin up databases, fork data, experiment, run simulations, and discard them — in seconds.

Neon saw this coming:

- 80% of its serverless Postgres databases are created by AI agents, not humans (Axios Pro Rata)
- It can spin up a fresh DB in under 500 ms, perfect for ephemeral, programmatic workflows
- Supports forking & branching like Git — ideal for experimentation without touching production

## Why Databricks + Neon makes perfect sense

Databricks already leads the AI model + data infra game. With Neon, it now owns a serverless, Postgres-native engine that’s tailored for how AI agents operate — fast, stateless, flexible.

This isn’t about SQL vs NoSQL or OLAP vs OLTP.

It’s about AI-native, ephemeral-first architecture.

## Why Snowflake + Crunchy Data matters

Snowflake didn’t sit still. It acquired Crunchy Data — an enterprise Postgres leader — to deliver Snowflake Postgres:

- Embedded in its AI Data Cloud
- Fully compliant, production-ready
- Built to scale agent-driven applications

## Connecting the dots

- Postgres is no longer just a “relational DB” — it’s extensible (with pgvector, HNSW, etc.), programmable, and AI-friendly
- AI agents need on-demand, forkable, cheap data infrastructure
- Databricks and Snowflake are building the AI OS — and Postgres is the data layer they trust

DBs aren’t static anymore. They’re live, ephemeral, spun up by agents, not humans.

We’re moving from “querying data” to “programming data systems for the AI age.”
