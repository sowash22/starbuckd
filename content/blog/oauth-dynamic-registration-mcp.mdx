OAuth with Dynamic Registration is the missing link in secure MCP for scaling AI agents.

## OAuth primer

You’ve a Google account, and you can log into Gmail to read your emails. That’s fine and dandy. But what if you want to let an AI agent or a third-party tool access your Gmail — maybe to summarize your inbox for the day or schedule a meeting or create action items?

You definitely don’t want to give it your Google username and password. That’s exactly what OAuth was built for.

OAuth is the protocol that lets applications securely access your data on your behalf, with your permission, and without sharing your login credentials.

If you’ve ever used “Login with Google” or “Sign in with Facebook” — that’s OAuth in action.

## A quick overview: how OAuth works

1. You visit a third-party app or AI agent that needs access to your Gmail, Jira, or calendar.
2. It redirects you to the identity provider — like Google, Microsoft, or Atlassian.
3. You log in and grant consent to allow limited access.
4. The identity provider sends back an authorization code.
5. The app exchanges that code for an access token.
6. That token is used to securely call APIs and retrieve data — like calendar events or Jira issues — on your behalf.

This lets apps and agents access your data safely, without ever seeing your password.

## Why OAuth is back in the spotlight: AI agents and MCP

Model Context Protocol (MCP) — an open standard by Anthropic — lets AI agents call external tools to fetch real-world context: documents, calendar events, emails, and more.

But MCP originally lacked something critical:

- A standardized, secure way to authorize agents to access user data.

## That’s where OAuth comes in

OAuth fills that gap — enabling agents to act on behalf of users securely, with explicit consent and well-scoped access.

## The scaling problem

Traditional OAuth requires every app or agent to be manually registered with each tool provider. That doesn’t scale in a world where agents are dynamically discovering new tools at runtime.

## The fix: dynamic client registration

The latest MCP update (2025-06-18) introduces OAuth Dynamic Client Registration, allowing agents to:

- Register themselves on the fly
- Skip manual setup
- Still use secure, standards-based OAuth flows

This unlocks:

- Seamless, secure agent-to-tool connections
- Scalable onboarding
- User consent + access control — without extra complexity and pre-setup

OAuth is now core to MCP, enabling AI agents to securely connect with tools — and dynamic registration makes it scalable and seamless.
