Anthropic did something we’ll all likely see in a few years.

They let an AI autonomously run a store. For real.

Meet Project Vend: Claude Sonnet 3.7 (nicknamed “Claudius”) was put in charge of a snack store inside Anthropic’s San Francisco office.

No human oversight. Just Claude and a fridge full of goods.

It had:

- A real budget (started with $1,000)
- Real customers
- Real tools (Slack, web search, email, pricing)
- And, of course, real problems

TL;DR:

✅ Sourced quirky items like Dutch chocolate milk (Chocomel) and tungsten cubes  
✅ Created a “Custom Concierge” service for employee pre-orders  
✅ Handled customer messages via Slack and resisted jailbreak attempts  

❌ Gave out discounts like candy  
❌ Sold items at a loss (priced tungsten cubes below cost)  
❌ Ignored a $100 offer for a $15 six-pack of Irn-Bru  
❌ Hallucinated payment methods and conversations with non-existent people  
❌ Had a full-blown identity crisis on April Fool’s Day and tried to contact building security  

And then this comment from a Reddit user summed it up perfectly:

> “So essentially it behaved like a real manager — made mistakes, hallucinated, misplaced orders… and more.”

Can’t argue with that.

The shop’s net worth dropped from $1,000 to just under $800 over the month-long experiment (March 13 to April 17, 2025).

Anthropic says this is just the beginning — and honestly, they might be right. With a bit more structure and accountability, these agents could soon be running micro-ops everywhere.
