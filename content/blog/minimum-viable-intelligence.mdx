We've seen it many times. Teams build impressive AI products super quick, demo it and work on productionalize it. But then something happens. It takes weeks and months for them to ship. What's the problem — what's slowing them down?

It turns out they apply traditional SLOs and SLAs. What they don’t understand is that AI systems are probabilistic by nature, and traditional constraints only slow down rollouts.

But companies disrupting the industry in this space operate differently — they iterate faster, trading strict SLAs for something more powerful: **Minimum Viable Intelligence (MVI)**.

Stable enough and intelligent enough to deliver value to customer even if it is not perfect and don't meet the traditional monitoring dashboard setups.

So the critical question we need to answer is: what level of variability can users tolerate while still delivering value from AI?

Replit offers a sharp example. When upgrading from Claude 3.5 to 3.7, they rebuilt their agent from scratch in three weeks, embraced unpredictability, and unlocked new capabilities. The result: a 10× revenue jump to roughly $100M ARR — not from higher uptime, but from giving users access to more possibility.

Anthropic’s Claude Code takes a similar stance. It was designed to be infinitely hackable — so users can poke, break, and bend it into new workflows. The value comes from the freedom to discover unexpected capabilities.

Recent OpenAI GTP5 release is another example — it is no where near perfect (in fact there are many backlashes) but still they delivered something many find it useful and they keep iterating to make it better.

The most successful AI products don’t aim for maximum control or perfect SLAs. They aim for Minimum Viable Intelligence — intelligent enough to surprise, stable enough to meet minimum thresholds, and open enough to let users discover what’s possible.
