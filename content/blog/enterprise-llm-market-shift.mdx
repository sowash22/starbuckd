Market share numbers usually blur together for me. But sometimes a data point cuts through the noise and connects to what you’re seeing in the trenches.

Enterprise LLM spending has more than doubled to $8.4B in just six months, with over half going to coding tasks — that's not surprising.

Naturally as you can predict Anthropic now has 32% of enterprise LLM market share because all major coding agents run on Anthropic models. This puts them ahead of OpenAI’s 20%. Two years ago OpenAI had 50% of this market. That’s a pretty dramatic flip.

But the above numbers don’t tell the whole story. The reality is more nuanced and it varies significantly between large enterprises, mid-market companies, and startups.

What's more interesting is: large enterprises (the Fortune 500s) are quietly moving toward on-premise open source models. Yes — back to on prem. Not for cost savings; they’ll pay premium for control and data privacy. They want Llamas running on their own steel because they can audit every token that touches their IP. Compliance isn’t negotiable when you’re dealing with regulated industries.

Mid-market companies are taking a different path entirely. They’re gravitating toward Claude or OpenAI for operational predictability. No rate limiting headaches. No surprise API changes. No midnight pages because the service is down. They want AI that works like their database — boring, reliable, always there.

Startups still default to OpenAI, mostly because the ecosystem assumed GPT from day one. Every tutorial, every integration, every Stack Overflow answer points to OpenAI. Switching has switching costs, even when better options exist.

The real divide isn’t about open versus closed source philosophy. It’s about control, compliance, risk tolerance and operational maturity.

Enterprises are choosing consistent 90% performance over brilliant-but-flaky 95% performance. They’ve learned that the bleeding edge cuts both ways.

In production systems, reliable and boring win. Every time.
