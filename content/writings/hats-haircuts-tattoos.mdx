Hats, haircuts, and tattoos. This isn't fashion advice.

You’ve seen it happen at work: multiple meetings and long threads on minor feature decisions. Product rollouts slip because everyone is stuck on small, reversible decisions. We treat every decision like it's mission-critical, and it kills our momentum.

What I've learned in recent times — and largely thanks to this simple yet effective model for solving analysis paralysis — is a framework Jeff Bezos used to keep Amazon moving fast: **The Two-Way Door Framework**.

## One-Way Doors

These are the big ones. The irreversible, high-consequence choices. Think a major architecture change, a public launch, or getting a tattoo. Once you walk through, there's no easy way back. For these, you must slow down and deliberate.

## Two-Way Doors

These are most decisions (~95%, in fact). They are reversible. If you make a mistake, you can walk right back through the door, learn from it, and try a different path.

Sounds simple right? But here is the trap where most people fall into. Most of us treat most two-way door like a one-way door and spend way too much time making a decision.

This is where hats haircuts and tattoos come in handy (inspired by James Clear):

- **Hats**: Easily reversible, low-cost decisions. Decide on the spot with the info you have. Pick one and go. If it doesn't work, change it.
- **Haircuts**: Reversible, but with temporary discomfort. Set a short deadline (an hour, a day), do quick research, and make the call.
- **Tattoos**: The permanent, painful-to-reverse decisions. Slow down, get advice, and think through consequences.

Taking too long to make a reversible decision can cost you opportunities, just as much as an irreversible mistake. Delaying action can lead to missed chances.

Action beats perfection.
