## The paradigm shift: from doers to simulators

While the AI community focuses on agents as “doers” (LLMs with tools for tasks), Nate argues the real opportunity lies in AI agents as “modelers” or “reality simulators.”

By adding a simulated world to the traditional LLM + tools + guidance stack, agents can become digital twins that explore alternate timelines, compress learning time, and compound insights.

## Three exponential value levers

### 1) Alternate timeline exploration

Simulating various options for business decisions, customer responses, or marketing campaigns before execution.

Example: A retail company runs 1,000 different pricing strategies in simulation, testing customer reactions, competitor responses, and market conditions before launching their actual campaign.

### 2) Time compression

Achieving hundreds of iterations in simulation time while competitors are still on early iterations.

Example: A pharmaceutical company simulates years of drug trials in weeks, testing molecular interactions and side effects across thousands of patient profiles without waiting for real-world trials.

### 3) Compounding insights

Developing better priors with each simulation, leading to nonlinear breakthroughs.

Example: A logistics company’s AI learns from each delivery route simulation, getting better at predicting traffic patterns, weather impacts, and customer availability, eventually discovering entirely new optimization strategies.

## Why this matters

The companies mastering simulation will significantly outpace those focused solely on automation. It’s about rewarding decision quality over outcomes and using simulation to avoid disasters before they happen.

This aligns with Jensen Huang’s recent CES 2025 keynote, where he demonstrated digital twins in action — noting that the audience was “inside NVIDIA’s digital twin” during his presentation. His focus on AI agents, robotics, and the new RTX Blackwell architecture shows how NVIDIA is building the infrastructure for this simulation-first future.

Real-world examples already proving this:

- Renault: cut vehicle development time by 60% using digital twins to predict crash outcomes
- BMW: built a virtual factory to simulate optimal line changes overnight
- Formula 1: real-time pit strategy simulations during races
- Ad networks: pre-testing creative mixes for uplift without spending money
