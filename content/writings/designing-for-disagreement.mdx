Lately, I’ve been using LinkedIn quite a bit more than usual and as someone who also spends a lot of time on places like Reddit, Hacker News, and Substack, I’ve noticed a stark contrast in product experience and people's culture between these.

Over there, people make posts and usually, deep discussions follow. If you're familiar with Hacker News or Reddit, comment threads are literal rabbit holes you'd happily dive into. That’s where the real value is. Ideas get challenged, refined, extended. People ask hard questions, share counterpoints, and build on each other’s thinking. It’s not always polished, but it’s productive.

On LinkedIn, it’s the opposite.

It’s post-heavy, but the dialogue is shallow. Comments are mostly generic but rarely offering any real intellectual take. Few people challenge ideas or offer alternate perspectives.

This is one of my biggest pet peeves with LinkedIn: I don’t get to see deeper dialogue. I’ve always loved seeing what people really think, how they push or question an idea. But here, I just keep scrolling because there’s rarely anything that pulls me in to think deeper. It starts feeling more like Instagram for professionals than a platform for learning through discourse.

And I get why: this is a professional network. Everything’s tied to your name, company, and title. Disagreeing publicly can feel risky, even when done respectfully.

So we default to agreement.

Or maybe — we just don’t have the time or energy to engage in nuanced debate through a keyboard between meetings.

And slowly, a platform full of sharp, experienced people turns into an echo chamber in a suit.

I wish there was more room here for thoughtful disagreement.

Not snark. Not takedowns. Just respectful friction — the kind that sharpens thinking and leads to better ideas.

And that got me thinking… what if LinkedIn made that kind of dialogue easier?

- An “Anonymity Mode” for questions or comments — so people can speak freely without fear of judgment
- A “Disagree” button — that registers dissent without naming names
- An AI bot that plays devil’s advocate — offering counterpoints in the comments to spark deeper conversation

These features wouldn’t just make conversations more interesting, they’d make them more valuable.

- They’d help us stress-test assumptions before they reach customers or stakeholders
- They’d lead to richer, more creative thinking — because great ideas often emerge from tension, not consensus
- They’d surface blind spots that only show up when someone pushes back
- And they’d normalize the kind of open, honest dialogue that drives real innovation

Maybe if someone from the LinkedIn product team sees this, they’ll help bring these ideas to life.
Or maybe… someone takes this idea and builds a new platform entirely.
