The browser wars are back — and this time, AI is leading the charge.

We’re witnessing the dawn of a new era in web browsing that feels remarkably similar to the dot-com boom of the late 90s. Just as Netscape challenged the status quo and sparked the first browser wars, today’s AI-powered browsers are poised to fundamentally reshape how we interact with the internet.

## What’s different this time (and why it matters)

This isn’t just another browser upgrade — it’s a shift from passive web consumption to active, intelligent assistance.

Perplexity’s Comet doesn’t just display web pages; it actively helps you understand, remember, and act on what you see. The browser can compare products across multiple sites, fill up shopping carts automatically, and guide users through entire purchase funnels in seconds. It’s like having a personal shopping assistant that never sleeps and knows every product on the internet.

OpenAI’s upcoming browser (reportedly launching within weeks) will likely integrate their AI agents directly into the browsing experience, potentially handling complex tasks like form filling, price negotiation, and trip planning.

The key difference: these browsers don’t just show you information — they can act on it.

## The 90s parallel

The original browser wars were full of unexpected twists.

Netscape pioneered commercial browsing and their 1995 IPO kicked off the dot-com boom — doubling on day one despite only $16M in revenue.

Internet Explorer crushed them through aggressive bundling with Windows, reaching 95% market share.

But success bred complacency. IE6 shipped in 2001 and wasn’t meaningfully updated until 2006, frustrating developers for years.

Google’s Chrome entry in 2008 was initially mocked — “another browser?” — but their secret weapon was treating each tab as a separate process. Chrome went from 0% to 70% market share in just a decade.

Microsoft’s Edge story is particularly humbling: after spending $100M+ developing their own engine, they couldn’t compete and rebuilt Edge on Chromium in 2018. It was essentially a white flag of surrender.

## There’s a hidden star

While AI gets all the attention, Chromium’s quiet dominance is enabling this revolution.

Here’s the twist: the real winner of the browser wars has been Chromium, Google’s open-source project. Today, Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera, and dozens of others all run on Chromium. Google gave away the engine and kept the data pipeline.

Most new AI browsers, including Perplexity’s Comet, are built on Chromium. This means AI browser creators can focus on intelligence rather than rebuilding web rendering from scratch. It’s like building a race car on a proven chassis.

Browser wars all over again — with bigger stakes than ever.
